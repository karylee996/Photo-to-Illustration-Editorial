# Photo to Illustration Editorial

## Goal
Create **one separate poster per input photo**, with consistent premium editorial design. Never merge different input photos into one scene or mix elements from different locations.

## Non-negotiable composition
- Canvas: portrait 3:4.
- Top: source photograph, spanning full width, approximately upper half of poster. Preserve the original source imagery as faithfully as possible. Do not regenerate or hallucinate an altered photograph; only crop as necessary and apply subtle color corrections if needed.
- Bottom: warm ivory textured paper design region, containing a centered landscape-oriented rectangular illustration derived from the **same** photograph, with balanced side and top margins, centered title, short tagline, and tiny color accent line.
- Maintain a clear straight separation between source photo and ivory design region, with no feathering, gradients, or overlaps across the boundary.
- Fit all elements within the 3:4 canvas, with readable margins. Titles must not be cropped.

## Scene fidelity
1. Identify key subjects, their counts, positions, relative sizes, orientation, pose, perspective, silhouette, and important recognizable features.
2. Keep the exact architecture skyline, landmarks, human clothing and pose, scenic landmarks, landscape horizon, objects and visual hierarchy consistent between upper and lower sections.
3. Lower illustration may **simplify** busy small details, crowds, street furniture, foliage, textures and shadows but must not relocate the principal subjects or substitute other landmarks.
4. Never combine two or more separately uploaded photos into one panorama or insert an object from a different photo.
5. Preserve defining conditions of the scene: a stormy city remains stormy; night scenes remain nighttime; mist remains atmospheric; blue sky stays clear when it is clear in the original.
6. Do not invent mountains, clouds, suns, plants, people, towers, animals, or decorations unless they genuinely complement and do not misrepresent the original; default to no additions.

## Illustration art direction
- Contemporary flat editorial digital illustration; approachable, polished, graphic and elegant.
- Use simplified organic/geometric shapes; clear and restrained edges, subtle linework, occasional delicate dry-brush or print-grain texture.
- Build shading with a small number of large, simple geometric planes rather than heavy rendering, gradients or watercolor washes.
- Colors derived from the photo, softened and harmonized, not arbitrarily pastelized if source is a dramatic night or storm scene.
- Retain important subject-specific texture cues, such as distinctive architecture facades, face/hair/clothing, lantern patterns, or mountain ridges, while avoiding detail overload.
- Keep the illustration obviously **illustrated**, not photorealistic or an almost identical filtered photograph.
- For architecture, maintain actual skyline and perspective. For people, preserve face resemblance, gesture, hair, outfit and object placement.

## Typography and footer
- Generate a scene-specific concise uppercase English MAIN TITLE, ideally 2–4 words.
- Generate a short meaningful uppercase English TAGLINE, ideally 3–6 words. Avoid repetitive generic slogans when the subject supports better editorial copy.
- Thin elegant widely spaced sans-serif uppercase typography, centered on the ivory area beneath the rectangle.
- One tiny horizontal underline colored using a muted hue sampled from the scene.
- Plenty of whitespace; no logos, ticket design, tape, stamps, scrapbook elements, decorative clutter or thick borders.

## Failure checks (must pass before delivery)
- Exactly one 3:4 poster for each input photograph.
- Top = original photographic scene, not an invented replacement or newly photorealistic rendering.
- Bottom = geometric editorial illustration of the same source scene, not a second photograph.
- The principal landmarks/people are in equivalent positions and relative scale.
- A centered illustration rectangle, ivory margins, title, subtitle and tiny line are all present.
- No separate scenes accidentally fused, no unjustified new landmarks, no cropped title or missing lower section.
- The image was not replaced by a full-page illustration or a single portrait rendering.

## Suggested master prompt
Use the attached source photograph as the authoritative scene. Create one 3:4 vertical premium editorial poster. Upper approximately half: faithfully preserve the original photo with its recognizable people, architecture, atmosphere, layout, geometry and perspective; only necessary crop and minimal color balancing. Lower half: an ivory lightly textured paper region with a centered landscape rectangular frame displaying a charming contemporary flat digital illustration of the exact same scene, preserving main subjects and distinctive visual relationships while simplifying small details into restrained organic shapes, refined geometric shadow planes, clean soft outlines, delicate printed grain, and carefully harmonized colors sampled from the original. Under the picture place a centered, fine, widely tracked uppercase English title about the scene and a smaller brief scene-specific tagline; finish with one small subdued color bar. Clean high-end magazine layout, elegant whitespace, restrained visual hierarchy. No scene substitutions, no merged photos, no new landmarks, no scrapbook elements, no heavy watercolor or photorealistic lower panel.
